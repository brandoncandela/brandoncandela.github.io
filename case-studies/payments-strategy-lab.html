Payments Strategy Lab

A tested unit-economics model that compares three approval strategies on revenue, fraud loss and review capacity, ending in a 90-day pilot memo.

Who it is for

Payments, risk and finance leaders deciding whether more approvals are worth the fraud and staffing cost.

What I built

A pure JavaScript financial model, sensitivity tables, a staffing payback analysis and a downloadable decision memo.

Result

Targeted review wins the base case, and the model shows that hiring to cover every review would cost $67K a month more than it saves.

The problem

A payments business wants to approve more legitimate purchases. Approving more also lets more fraud through and sends more payments to a review team with fixed capacity. The decision needs numbers a CFO and a risk lead both accept.

Architecture

How data moves from source to decision.

  1. AssumptionsVolume, ticket size, fraud rate, margin and review headcount.
  2. ModelPure functions compute approvals, fraud loss, review cost and contribution per strategy.
  3. StressSensitivity across fraud rates and a staffing payback scenario.
  4. DecidePick a strategy, write the reasoning, export the memo.

Technologies

  • JavaScript (pure model module)
  • node:test
  • HTML and CSS

Engineering decisions

Keep the model pure

No DOM in the model, so it can be tested on its own and reused.

Check against an independent calculation

A reference case computed separately guards the formulas.

Capacity overflow is declined

Payments the team has no time to review are declined, which makes the staffing tradeoff explicit.

Tests

7 node:test cases, run with one command and no network.

  • An independently calculated reference case
  • The cash bridge reconciles
  • Capacity limits reviews, and spare staff cost money without adding approvals
  • Zero fraud gives zero fraud loss; invalid inputs are rejected

Results

  • Base case: targeted review $479,342, conservative $464,660, growth first $388,364 monthly contribution
  • Full review coverage needs 25 FTE; adding 13 costs $67,073 a month net

Limitations

  • All inputs are fictional, not benchmarks
  • Contribution excludes overhead, tax, capital and lifetime value
  • A scenario model, not a forecast

What I would build next
